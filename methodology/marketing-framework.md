# Marketing Framework

## The foundation: Seth Godin's marketing structure

People don't buy products. They buy change — a story about who they'll become. The marketer's job isn't to change minds but to find people who already want to change and offer them a path.

Three elements:

**The Change** — what you're actually offering. Not a service, not a product, but a transformation. For us: *freedom to operate, at lower cost*. The client goes from vendor-dependent to in control.

**The Worldview** — the beliefs your audience already holds. You don't create the worldview, you speak to it. Our audience already believes: "my systems should work for me, not for my vendors." They feel the pain of surprise pricing increases, licensing changes, vendor roadmap dependency. They want control but don't know how to get it.

**The Story** — proof that the change is real and achievable. Not a pitch, but evidence that someone like them made the change and came out stronger. Case studies, articles, community examples. The story says: "people like you did this. Here's what happened."

**The smallest viable audience** — not everyone. The specific people whose worldview aligns with the change you offer.

## The Mycorrhizal Method: delivery mechanism

The sales methodology defines how the marketing framework reaches the audience through human interaction.

**Soil** — the stories exist in the landscape. They build the worldview before any conversation happens.
**Signal** — someone who holds the worldview does something visible.
**Reframe** — a hunter articulates the change in the prospect's specific context.
**Diagnosis** — the assessment proves the change is possible and quantifies it.
**Proposal** — the offer to guide the change.
**Sustain** — the change is real. The client tells the story to others.

## How they work together

```
                    MARKETING FRAMEWORK (what to say, to whom)
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │                                      │
                    │   The Change ──→ The Worldview       │
                    │       ↕               ↕              │
                    │   The Story ←── The Audience         │
                    │                                      │
                    └───────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                                    │
                    ┌───────────────▼──────────────────────┐
                    │                                      │
                    │   SALES METHOD (how to say it, when) │
                    │                                      │
                    │   Soil ──→ Signal ──→ Reframe        │
                    │    ↑                      │          │
                    │    │                      ▼          │
                    │   Sustain ←── Proposal ←── Diagnosis │
                    │                                      │
                    └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The marketing framework provides the content. The sales method provides the choreography. They form a loop:

1. **The Story** (marketing) creates **Soil** (method) — content that builds the worldview
2. **The Audience** (marketing) emits **Signals** (method) — people whose worldview aligns do visible things
3. **The Change** (marketing) becomes the **Reframe** (method) — articulated for one specific person
4. **The Worldview** (marketing) shapes the **Diagnosis** (method) — the assessment speaks to what the prospect already believes
5. **Sustain** (method) produces a new **Story** (marketing) — client success feeds back into the landscape

## Three source types

The central intelligence organizes content by the role it plays in this framework:

### Foundation

Who we are, what we promise, who we're for. This is Seth's domain — the change and the worldview.

**Sources:** Marketing repository, aknostic.com.
**Extracts:** Change statements, worldview records (beliefs, pains, readiness signals), personas, competitive positioning.
**Changes:** Slowly. Foundational positioning evolves over months.

### Narrative

Proof the change is real. Stories that resonate with the audience's worldview.

**Sources:** Clouds of Europe articles, aknostic.com blog and case studies, marketing repository.
**Extracts:** Insights and reframes — the change articulated as a perspective shift a hunter can use in conversation.
**Changes:** Frequently. Every new article, every new case study adds to the evidence.

### Conversation

What happens in practice. The live loop from the Mycorrhizal Method.

**Sources:** Hunters and gatherers through daily work — meeting debriefs, signal observations, follow-up notes, retrospectives.
**Not extracted from external sources.** Entered by humans. This is where the method meets reality, and where the feedback loop generates new stories and new insights.

### Two lenses, not two buckets

A single piece of content — a blog post, a case study, a positioning page — often contains both foundation and narrative material. The ingestion pipeline handles this by running the same content through two independent extraction lenses. A case study on aknostic.com yields personas and competitive positioning (foundation) *and* reframes with evidence (narrative). The lenses have different cadences: foundation refreshes after significant positioning changes, narrative runs on a recurring schedule to catch new stories as they appear.

## Implications for training

Training follows the framework layers, top to bottom:

1. **Start with the change** — the foundational instruction. What do we offer? Why does it matter? Generated from the `change` table.
2. **Teach the worldview** — who are we talking to? What do they believe? What signals readiness? Generated from `worldview` + `personas`.
3. **Practice the reframes** — the change articulated for one person in their context. Generated from `insights` + `personas`.
4. **Support with stories** — connect reframes to proof. Generated from `case_studies` + narrative insights.
5. **Reinforce through conversation** — daily rhythm, meeting prep, debrief. Generated from `interactions` + `signals` + live data.

New content at any layer automatically refreshes training. A new Clouds of Europe article becomes a narrative insight, which becomes a training exercise, within hours of publication.
